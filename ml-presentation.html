Definition

What is
Machine Learning?

Machine learning is a field of artificial intelligence that gives computers the ability to learn from data and improve their performance on tasks — without being explicitly programmed for each one.

  • Data-driven — learns from examples rather than hand-crafted rules
  • Pattern recognition — finds hidden relationships in large, complex datasets
  • Self-improving — performance increases with more data and feedback
02 — 05

Taxonomy

Three Paradigms of Learning

I
Supervised Learning
Trained on labeled examples. The model learns to map inputs to known outputs, then predicts on new data.
e.g. Spam filters, image classifiers, price prediction
II
Unsupervised Learning
No labels provided. The model discovers hidden structure and natural groupings within the data itself.
e.g. Customer segmentation, anomaly detection, topic modeling
III
Reinforcement Learning
An agent learns by taking actions, receiving rewards or penalties, and optimizing its long-term strategy.
e.g. Game-playing AI, robotics, recommendation tuning
03 — 05

Process

How a Model Learns

1
Collect & Prepare
Gather raw data, clean inconsistencies, handle missing values, and engineer meaningful features.
2
Choose & Train
Select an algorithm, feed it training data, and let it iteratively adjust internal parameters to minimize error.
3
Evaluate
Test on unseen data using metrics like accuracy, precision, or RMSE. Tune hyperparameters to close gaps.
4
Deploy & Monitor
Serve predictions in production. Monitor for drift, retrain periodically as real-world distributions shift.
